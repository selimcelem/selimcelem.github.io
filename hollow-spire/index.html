the climb

Death is expected

Every door is a choice. Every tenth floor holds a gate-keeper. When an heir falls, the souls they gathered return to the Manor, the gold feeds the Armory, and the next heir climbs stronger. The summit stands on floor 50 at first. Each ascension makes the Spire harder. From Ascension 4 the summit rises too, to floor 200 at Ascension 10, where the Hollow King waits.

The bloodline

Each generation draws new heirs with their own traits. Whatever one dies holding can pass down as an heirloom.

Sealed hands

Relics drop as a hand of sealed cards. Break the seals, take one, the others burn. Of the 124 relics, 41 change a rule rather than a number.

Two dice a swing

Your heir swings on their own, and two dice roll with every swing. A high roll hits harder, and a roll at your crit number or above is a crit. Cast an ability at the peak of a swing and it lands Perfect.

Break the Barrier

From Ascension 4, every gate-keeper stands behind a Barrier that turns away 25% to 50% of your damage. A ring fills around the Break button; tap Break when the ring reaches 100% to wear the Barrier down.

between climbs

The Manor

The Manor belongs to the whole line: what one heir buys there, every heir after keeps.

The Manor

Souls buy its ranks. A rank is kept by every heir who comes after.

The Armory

Gold buys the line's gear: a weapon, armor and a charm, one tier at a time. Every heir carries all three.

The Vault

A felled gate-keeper can leave a vestige. The Vault keeps every vestige you find, and each heir wears one Arms, one Ward and one Sign on every climb.

Bellows & Hammer

Choose a piece, then practice the work for free before spending gold. Heat, three blows, quench: each in gold is Perfect. Five Perfect marks make a Masterwork.

Ways to climb

Each ascension leaves an Eclipse Sigil. Spend a Sigil to bind a boon of your choice to the bloodline. Three of the boons are new ways to climb.

floor 200 and after

Take the Crown

At Ascension 10 the Hollow King waits on floor 200. Beat him, then choose what your heir does next. Climb Beyond goes on past floor 200. Return to the Manor ends the climb with the victory recorded. Take the Crown ends the climb too, and begins a Prestige.

In a Prestige the line starts again at Ascension 0. It loses its Manor, Armory, vestiges, gold, souls and heirs, and keeps its Prestige stars, the Codex and its history. Each Prestige star gives foes 15% more health and 10% more attack, and makes every climb pay 25% more gold and souls. Every Prestige starts with 3 Eclipse Sigils for each Prestige star. From Prestige 1 the relic pool grows by 21.

Crowned heirs

Through every Prestige the line remembers the heirs who beat the Hollow King. These are its crowned heirs. In a later Prestige, one of them can meet your heir at the start of a climb, and the first meeting plays as a short painted comic. Take the crowned heir along as an ally, and the ally takes half of that climb's gold and souls. Or duel the crowned heir: a win brings a vestige and an Eclipse Sigil, and a loss kills your heir.

The night my father died

This book belongs to Aurel, eldest son of King Othmar.

My father died tonight, a little after the second bell. I want to remember this night exactly, so I am writing it down now.

He could not speak at the end. He held my hand, and then he held my brother Khorvax's hand, and then he let go of both of us.

The servants have put a black cloth over the throne. The cloth stays there until the council names a king. Khorvax says the soldiers will name him, and he is probably right. The soldiers love him. I understand why.

I walked past the throne on my way to bed. I am not afraid of my brother. I am afraid of that throne under its black cloth, with nobody on it.

IIThe empty throneAlways in the book

Winter. Forty days without a king

Forty days, and the council has named no one. Half of them want Khorvax, half want me, and neither half will give way.

The farmers expect a war. They have hidden their grain from the soldiers, and the town is already hungry.

This afternoon our cousin Pell walked into the throne room, pulled off the black cloth and sat down on the throne. He waved at the people who came to look. The guards let him sit there for an hour while they waited for someone to tell them what to do.

Tonight the bellmaster rang the great alarm bell for the first time in my life. He told me he rings it whenever someone tries to take the throne.

Tomorrow I will ask Khorvax to share the rule with me. I do not know what he will say.

IIIMy brotherThe keeper of floor 10

The first day of spring

Khorvax is dead. I killed him in the throne room this morning.

Our war lasted nineteen days. He would not share the rule, and I would not give it up. On the first day he took our father's gold circlet from the treasury and wore it, and his soldiers cheered him. This morning he came into the throne room wearing it, with the sword Caldus made for him. I carried the one Caldus made for me. When we were boys, Father made us fight with wooden swords on the chapel steps, and Khorvax always won.

He fell on the steps of the throne. He said my name once. I think he wanted to say something else.

I would not let the soldiers bury him. Two men of the guard carried him down to the crypt under the first floor of the keep, and the three of us dug the grave.

Before we filled it in, I put the circlet back on his head. He wore it for nineteen days. He can keep it.

IVMother MyrraThe keeper of floor 20

Summer, the first year of my reign

Khorvax's soldiers burned the fields when they fell back in the spring. There will be no harvest this year.

Old Myrra has fed the town since spring. She grows mushrooms on wooden beds in the old silver mine under the hill, as her mother did. The children of the town call her Mother Myrra, and every morning they follow her down the mine road with baskets.

I went down with her today. The air in the mine is warm and wet and smells of earth. Her beds go back further than the lamps reach.

I offered her a room in the keep and a pension. She said she would rather stay near her beds, and asked me to pay the pension in salt.

VThe sea gatesThe keeper of floor 30

Autumn, the first year

Three grain ships came in from the south today. We will eat this winter.

The ships unload in the sea caves under the keep, behind the sea gates. Ulhet keeps the gates. He has kept them for thirty years, and his father kept them before him.

He walked me along the gallery above the water and showed me the tide marks cut into the rock, the oldest at the bottom and each new one higher. He says the sea would have taken these halls a hundred years ago if nobody kept the gates, and he checks every gate himself each morning.

I envy him. He knows exactly what his work is for.

V

A later scrap, found beside the sea gates

I ordered the sea gates shut, and Ulhet shut them. Now I have ordered them opened, and he will not open them. He walks the gallery above the water and checks every gate each morning, the way he always did.

VICaldusThe keeper of floor 40

Autumn, the first year. The night before my crowning

Our father's circlet is in the crypt with Khorvax, so Caldus has made me a new one. He brought it up from the forge this evening, still warm, and set it on my table without a word.

Caldus made each of us a sword on our sixteenth birthday, two years apart. Years later I asked him which sword was better. He said he had made them equally well, and he would not choose between us.

The men call him the Furnace-King, because in the forge even my captains take his orders. Tomorrow a priest will crown me before the court.

I tried the new circlet on after Caldus left. It fits.

VI

A later scrap, found in the forge

I ordered Caldus to arm every man in the keep. He is still at it. The men are gone, and the swords stand in rows along the forge walls, and he makes more. He made my brother's sword and mine, and he would never say which was better.

VIIMy stewardThe summit of Ascension 0

The third year of my reign

My steward has run this keep since my father's time. He knows the price of salt, the name of every guard, and which of my councillors are lying. I have never once seen him sit down.

The servants call him the Sovereign when they think I cannot hear. They mean it as a joke, but they are right: the keep runs as well when I am away as when I am here.

Tonight I told him he served me better than I had earned. He looked embarrassed.

Ilse, who will be my wife in the spring, says I should give him a title. I asked her which one. She said: anything but Sovereign.

VII

A later scrap, found at the top of the old tower

When the keep began to grow, my steward climbed the new stair to reach me. He carried my order with him: nobody climbs without my word. He stands where the old keep's tower used to end, and stops everyone who comes. He has not sat down since.

VIIIThe gardensAscension 1, the keeper of floor 10

Spring, the fourth year

Ilse and I were married today, in the gardens under the east wall.

The old gardener planted white roses along the path and white lilies around the fountain, so that we could see them after the candles were lit. Under the rose beds lie some of the people who died in the forty days: that winter the gardens were the only ground soft enough to dig. On her way to the vows, Ilse stopped at each of their graves.

During the vows I put the ring on the wrong hand. Ilse laughed so hard that the priest had to wait for her.

I have never been so happy. I am writing it all down tonight so that I never forget it.

VIII

A later scrap, found in the gardens

The gardener still keeps the gardens under the east wall. The Spire sends the souls of its dead home, but it keeps their bones, and the gardener buries them under the roses. The bones will not stay there. Every morning he buries them again.

IXThe marshAscension 1, the keeper of floor 20

Summer, the fifth year

Ilse is carrying our first child. The midwife says the child will come in the autumn.

This evening we walked in the salt marsh under the landward wall. The pools there are so still that the whole keep hangs upside down in them, with the two of us beside it.

We stopped at the largest pool. Ilse put my hand on her stomach and told me to wait. I felt nothing, and said so, and she told me to wait again.

Once, while we waited, I saw my reflection look up a moment before I did. I told myself I had imagined it.

We stood there until the light went. Then I felt the child move.

IX

A later scrap, found in the marsh

Bram writes from the marsh: one of my blood fought a stranger there with the same face, the same sword and the same way of moving. The heir won, and the stranger sank into the pool. I remember my reflection looking up before I did.

XCandlesAscension 1, the keeper of floor 30

Autumn, the fifth year

Our son Bram was born this morning. He is red-faced and loud and entirely well. Ilse is well too, and asleep.

Pyre, who makes the chapel's candles, came to our room with a candle as long as my arm. Whenever someone in the keep is born or dies, he lights a candle for them in the chapel. He has done it for forty years, and he says he has never once run short of wax.

He lit Bram's candle in the same row as the candles he lit for my father and for my brother.

I did not know he had lit one for Khorvax. I stood in the chapel a long time.

A later scrap, found in the candle rooms

Pyre still lights a candle for everyone who dies in the keep. The Spire is still my keep, so every heir who dies on its stair gets a candle. His rows of candles reach the ceiling now. He has never once run short of wax.

XIThe abbotAscension 1, the keeper of floor 40

Winter, the eighth year

Storms sank the grain ships in the autumn. This is the hungriest winter since the forty days.

The abbot preached today that the hunger is a punishment: for the forty days without a king, and for my killing Khorvax. He did not look at me while he said it. Everyone else did.

I was angry, and I sent for him. He came, and he was so thin that I could not shout at him. He has eaten nothing but broth since the first snow. He says he will not eat bread while families in the town go without it.

I sent him a loaf. He gave it to a family at the chapel door.

XI

A later scrap, found in the chapel

The abbot died of his fast in the hungry winter, long before I put on the Crown. Bram writes that he is in the chapel again, preaching to empty benches, thinner than he ever was. The Crown brought him back. He is still hungry.

XIIThe clockThe summit of Ascension 1

Spring, the ninth year

The horologist has finished the great clock in the east tower. It took him six years. It strikes the hours on the bellmaster's bells, and it is the most beautiful thing in the keep.

I asked him if he could make a clock that would never stop. He said someone would always have to wind it and mend the worn parts.

I asked what would happen to the clock when he died. He said he was already teaching an apprentice, a girl from the town with quick hands, and that she would keep it better than he did.

He did not seem worried at all.

A later scrap, found in the clock tower

The great clock in the east tower has stopped, and I can still hear it ticking. The horologist works on it day and night. He trained an apprentice to take it over after him. The Crown will not let him hand it to her.

Summer, the tenth year

Ilse will not let me win an argument just because I am king. Tonight she told me I am afraid of furniture.

She meant the throne. I had been telling her again about the forty days, and the black cloth, and Pell waving from the throne for an hour. She said: Aurel, you have three healthy children and a full granary, and you are frightened of a chair.

XVThe feverThe summit of Ascension 4

Winter, the twenty-second year

The fever has been in the keep for a month. It killed the provisioner's wife and two men of the guard. Now I have it.

Ser Oren has guarded the children's rooms since the first day. He lets nobody in who has been near the sick, not even me, and he sleeps sitting up in the corridor.

Today I made Bram regent, and the council agreed that he will be king if I die. He is seventeen. If I die in the night, the throne will not stand empty for one hour.

Bram knelt by my bed and promised to rule well. I believe him. Greta stood at the back of the room. When I asked her to come closer, she stayed where she was.

XV

A later scrap, found in the great hall

Bram still signs his reports as regent. He was to be regent until I died, and king after me. I have not died. He does not sleep, and he will not stop ruling a keep that has emptied around him.

XVIThe feastAscension 5, the keeper of floor 70

Spring, the twenty-third year

I am well again. The provisioner gave a feast of eleven courses for my recovery, in the painted hall under the ceiling with the stars on it. His wife died in the fever. I think he needed something to do with his hands.

At supper Greta told the table that Bram had stayed regent for a month after I was well, and that she would have handed the rule back the morning I could stand. Bram said I had chosen him, not her. Greta said I had never once thought of asking her.

Everyone laughed as if it were a joke. I laughed too. Bram and Greta did not.

XVI

A later scrap, found in the painted hall

I ordered my table laid every night. The provisioner lays it under the painted stars, eleven courses, and carries every dish away untouched. Nobody comes to eat. He will not stop serving.

XVIIThe book of kingsThe summit of Ascension 5

Summer, the twenty-third year

I went down to the Archive and asked the archivist why my father kept the iron Crown, the oldest crown in the house, locked away and never wore it.

She brought me the book of kings. It is older than our family. Each entry names a king who wore the iron Crown, the one thing he asked it for, and what came after. A king who wears the Crown on the throne may ask it for one thing, she said, and what the Crown grants lasts forever.

I read eleven entries. One king asked never to feel hunger again. He starved, because he could no longer tell when he needed to eat.

In the margin beside that entry, in my father's hand, are five words: Do not listen to the Crown.

Those kings asked the Crown to change them. I would only ask it to keep my children from a war. I keep telling myself that is different.

The last page of the book is blank.

XVII

A later scrap, found in the Archive

I ordered every day of my reign written down. Bram found the archivist in the Archive, writing. She has filled every book on the shelves. The book of kings lies open beside her at its last page, and that page is still blank.

XVIIIThe bellsAscension 6, the keeper of floor 90

Autumn, the twenty-fourth year

The great alarm bell rang this afternoon. I ran into the yard with half the guard behind me.

Bram and Greta were fighting there with drawn swords, over whether Bram could keep her off the council. His lip was cut. Her sleeve was red to the elbow. Neither of them lowered a sword until I stood between them.

The bellmaster told me afterwards that he rings the great bell when someone is coming for the throne. He did not see why it should matter who.

Ilse had Greta's arm dressed and sent her to the old hunting lodge at the foot of the hill until spring. Bram wanted me to bar Greta from the council. I refused, and he has not spoken to me since.

Tonight I put my hand on the steps of the throne, where Khorvax fell.

XVIII

A later scrap, found in the bell tower

I ordered the great bell rung for anyone on the stair. The bellmaster is gone, and the bell rings anyway, every time one of you starts to climb. In my father's time it rang for anyone who came for the throne. I made it ring for my own blood.

XIXThe iron CrownThe summit of Ascension 6

Winter, the twenty-fourth year

Tonight I took the iron Crown out of the Archive. The archivist asked me whether Ilse knew. I said I would tell her.

When I lifted the Crown out of its case, I heard my name, in my brother Khorvax's voice. I looked at the door before I understood that the voice came from the iron. So that is what my father meant: Do not listen to the Crown.

The Crown is heavier than it looks, and colder. When I turned it toward the fire, the black iron shone for a moment like old gold. It said nothing more.

I did not put it on. I have hidden it in my desk, and I have not told Ilse.

XIX

A later scrap, found in the old throne room

Some nights the Crown lifts off my head by itself and goes down to the old throne room, where the kings were crowned. It walks there burning like gold, with nobody under it, and it always comes back to me. While it is gone I still do not sleep. I sleep only when one of you takes it from me.

the grimoire

What waits on the stair

Five zones of ten floors, each with its own foes, an elite that winds up heavy blows, and a gate-keeper at the tenth floor. The lines are the Grimoire's own.

Floors 1 to 10The Undercroft

Floors 11 to 20The Fungal Deep

Floors 21 to 30The Drowned Halls

Floors 31 to 40The Ember Foundry

Floors 41 to 50The Astral Rift

On odd ascensions the first fifty floors are other halls: The Ossuary Gardens, The Mirror Marsh, The Chandler's Halls, The Starving Church, The Clockwork Abyss. Above floor 50 the Spire rises through The Hollow Crown, The Star Larder, The Blood Archive, The Bell Foundries, The Roofless Throne. Past floor 100 the halls repeat, and every foe returns stronger as an Echoing foe. The foes of all ten halls are left for you to find.
